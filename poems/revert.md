# Revert

It wasn't wrong, exactly — it just wasn't ours.
A stanza landed where a paragraph grew.
So I typed the command that unwinds the hours:
`revert` — not anger, just *back to what we knew.*

It named a version the way you name a door:
v7, the room where the words were still mine.
It saved what to put back, then asked no more;
the claim came with it, folded, by design.

But a revert is never an eraser's swipe.
You *apply* what it saved, you say why, you sign —
a put with `--revert`, an honest wipe:
the log keeps both: the turn and the line.
